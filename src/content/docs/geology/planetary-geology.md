# Concepts

- flood volcanism / flood basalt / plateau basalt
  - result of a giant volcanic eruption of basalt lava that covers a large stretch of land or ocean floor
  - basalt -> formed from the rapid cooling of low viscosity lava rich in iron or magnesium (mafic lava)
    - more than 90% of all volcanic rock on Earth is basalt

# Mercury

- period of rotation is 58 Earth days
- despite its proximity to the Sun, temperatures on its polar plains does not exceed -106 C. Inside its craters, where sunlight cannot reach, temperatures drop as low as - 171 C.

## Core
- solid iron-rich core (60% of planet's volume)
- probably one or more iron-rich molten layers surrounding the core
  - evidence: 20% shift north in magnetic equator (largest of any planet) indicates dynamo effect

## Surface
- basaltic rock and smooth plains
  - result of flood volcanism
  - volcanic period ended when the mantle contracted enough to disallow any volcanic material from reaching the surface
- impact craters
- compression folds
  - thought to be caused by the contraction of the planet's interior as it cooled, which in turn caused the surface to deform
  - might also be caused by the sudden disappearance of subsurface volatiles (elements that can easily transform from solid to liquid or gas) due to intrusion of magma from below
    - one such volatile is water (although it is unlikely water existed on Mercury)
      - subsurface water on Mercury would exist in a temperature zone cool enough to support life despite its proximity to the Sun
- tidal bulges
  - caused by the Sun's tides (17% stronger than the Moon's tides on Earth)
