title: Earth's Atmosphere
---

- ~78% Nitrogen, ~21% oxygen, 1% a bunch of other gases

## Stratification

- Exosphere (436 - 6,214 miles)
  - low densities of Hydrogen and small amounts of Helium, Carbon Dioxide, and Oxygen
  - atoms and molecules are so far apart it no longer behaves like a gas
  - particles constantly escape into space
  - contains many of the artificial satellites that orbit Earth
- Thermosphere (50 - 435 miles)
  - temperature actually increases with height due to absorption of ionizing UV and x-rays from the Sun
  - contains most of the artificial satellites orbiting Earth, specifically the International Space Station
- Mesosphere (31 - 50 miles)
  - coldest place on Earth
  - absorbs sound waves
  - where most meteors and satellites burn up when entering atmosphere
  - too high for jets, too low for orbitals
- Stratosphere (7 - 31 miles)
  - 99% of the atmosphere's mass lies below the 19 mile mark
  - contains the ozone layer
  - temperature increases with height because the ozone layer absorbs UV
    - this restricts turbulence and mixing, meaning its almost completely free of clouds and other forms of weather
  - unique to Earth, neither Mars nor Venus has a Stratosphere (not enough oxygen)
  - highest layer accessible to jets
- Troposphere (0 - 7 miles)
  - temperature declines with height because this layer is mostly heated by surface radiation
  - contains ~80% of the atmosphere's mass
  - contains nearly all atmospheric water vapor
    - thus, weather happens here
    - the ability of the atmosphere to retain water decreases with temperature, so 90% of the water vapor is in the lower part of this layer

### Other layers

- ozone layer
  - contained withing the stratosphere
  - peak concentration of ozone within atmosphere
- ionosphere
  - region of atmosphere ionized by solar radiation
  - 31 - 621 miles, including the mesosphere, thermosphere, exosphere
    - ionization in the mesosphere mostly ceases at night
  - located just below the plasmasphere, which is the inner magnetosphere consisting of low-energy (cool) plasma
    - plasma is a state of matter resulting from a gaseous state that's undergone some degree of ionization, meaning it consists of a significant portion of charged particles
- homosphere
  - gases are mixed due to turbulence
  - troposphere, stratosphere, mesosphere, lowest part of thermosphere
- heterosphere
  - gases are separated by weight, varying the chemical composition with altitude
  - significantly less dense so particles can move large distances without colliding, negating the mixing effect of turbulence
- planetary boundary layer
  - part of troposphere closest to Earth's surface
  - well-mixed during the day, at night it becomes stably stratified with weak mixing
- barosphere
  - region of the atmosphere where the barometric law applies, meaning pressure decreases as height increases
  - troposphere, stratosphere, mesosphere
- sodium layer
  - sporadic layer of neutral sodium atoms lying in the range of 50 - 65 miles with a thickness of 3 miles
  - the sodium comes from the ablation (vaporization) of meteors
