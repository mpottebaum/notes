# Venus

- no intrinsic magnetic field
- no plate tectonics
  - stagnant lid crust
  - significant presence of nitrogen in the atmosphere could indicate there were plate tectonics at one time
    - in rocky planets, nitrogen is trapped in minerals until released by volcanic activity
    - geochemical reactions of plate tectonics could have buried a lot of the carbon dioxide that today makes Venus so inhospitable to life
  - alternative theory: plutonic squishy lid
    - magma intrudes the outer crust (lithosphere) without breaking it into plates
- no asthenosphere
- period of rotation: 243.7 Earth days

## Core

- thought to have a core similar to Earth: liquid iron-nickel surrounding solid iron-nickel
  - yet, Venus does not have a magnetic field
    - implication: it does not have convection
      - convection occurs when there is a large difference in temperature between the inner and outer core
      - because Venus has no plate tectonics, heat in the mantle is not released and this warm mantle suppresses convection


## Surface
- 75% rock, primarily volcanic rock
- many deformations likely due to volcanism, mantle convection (i.e., hot stuff coming up from below)
  - coronae
    - collapsed lava domes resembling crowns
  - arachnoids
    - of unknown origin
    - name comes from resemblance to spider webs
  - pancake dome
    - between 10-100 times larger than lava domes found on Earth
- total distance between lowest and highest point is about 8 miles, which is about the same as on Earth (between the ocean floor and the highest summits of the Himalayas)https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planetary_differentiation
- the surface is rather young, meaning the surface has recently (geologically speaking) undergone resurfacing
  - the evidence is the relatively small amount of observed craters
  - theories are either one global resurfacing event occurring about 300-500 million years ago or a cyclic, continuous resurfacing
    - the idea is the interior heats up (due to decay of radioactive elements) to a critical point (because there are no plates, just a solid crust) at which point lava forces its way to the surface and covers the entire surface
    - whether its one single event or continuous resurfacing depends at least partly on whether Venus is still geologically active
