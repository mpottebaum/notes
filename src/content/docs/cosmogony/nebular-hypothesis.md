## Star Formation

- stars form in massive, dense clouds of primarily molecular hydrogen
  - when a portion of the cloud reaches a critical size, mass, or density, it collapses under its own gravity and forms a star
- star formation always produces a protoplanetary disk, a rotating disk of dense gas and dust, but protoplanetary disks don't always produce planets
  - thus, planet formation is a natural result of star formation
- the initial collapse takes about 100,000 years and a Sun-like star takes a total of about 1 million years to form

## Protoplanetary Disk

- takes from 10 million - 100 million years to evolve into a planet system
- protoplanetary disk is an accretion disk, a structure formed by diffuse material in orbital motion around a massive central body
  - friction, uneven irradiance, magnetohydrodynamic effects, and other forces cause orbiting material to spiral inward toward the central body
  - in this way, the protoplanetary disk "feeds" the star
- at the T Tauri Star stage (post-collapse but less than 10 million years old), the disk is cool enough for the formation of dust grains made of rock and ice
  - these grains might eventually coagulate into kilometer-sized planetesimals
    - the kilometer mark is important because at this size the body's constituent grains can attract each other directly through mutual gravity
  - if the disk is massive enough, runaway accretions begin forming Moon- to Mars-sized planetary embryos (also called protoplanets)
    - planetary embryos are astronomical bodies that have undergone internal melting to produce a differentiated interior

## Planet Formation

- once it's accumulated enough mass, a protoplanet assumes a spherical shape (due to hydrostatic pressure), becoming a dwarf planet
- to be considered a planet, a dwarf planet must "clear the neighborhood"
  - this means it has become gravitationally dominant; there are no bodies of comparable size in its orbital zone other than its natural satellites or those under its gravitational influence

## Rocky planets

- form within the frost line
  - minimum distance from a central protostar where temperature is low enough for volatile compounds (such as water, ammonia, methane, carbon dioxide, carbon monoxide) to condense into solid grains
- thus, formed from purely rocky grains and rock planetesimals

### Stages of Formation

- runaway accretion
  - occurs after planetesimals about 1 km in diameter form
  - the growth rate increases as mass increases and larger bodies stop the further growth of smaller ones
- oligarchic accretion
  - several hundred of the largest bodies (the oligarchs) dominate growth and accrete smaller planetesimals
  - accretion rate slows with growth, allowing smaller oligarchs to catch up
  - oligarchs accrete until all planetesimals in the surround area are exhausted
      - nearby oligarchs sometimes merge as well
  - isolation mass - final mass of an oligarch, which depends on its distance from the central star and the surface density of its planetesimals
    - for rocky planets, this is up to one Mars mass
  - final result of this stage is the formation of about 100 Moon- to Mars-sized protoplanets uniformly spaced
- merger stage
  - protoplanets are massive enough to perturb each other, causing their orbits to become chaotic
    - they expel any remaining planetesimals and collide with each other
  - results in 2-5 Earth-sized planets
