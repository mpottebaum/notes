---
title: Water Column
---

concept to describe the physical (temperature, salinity, light penetration) and chemical (pH, dissolved oxygen, utrient salts) characteristics of seawater at different depths

## Common Parameters

- pH
- turbidity
  - cloudiness or haziness of a liquid caused by large numbers of particles individually invisible to the naked eye
- temperature
- hydrostatic pressure
  - pressure at a given point exerted by the weight of a fluid column above it
- salinity
- total dissolved solids
- pesticides
- pathogens
- chemicals
- biota

## Pelagic Zones

### Epipelagic

- depth: 0 - 656 feet
- AKA sunlit zone or photic zone or euphotic zone
- depth of water penetrated by sunlight
    - allowing phytoplankton to perform photosynthesis
      - as a result, most aquatic life lives in this zone
- thickness varies with season, latitude, and degree of turbidity
  - turbidity can be strongly driven by phytoplankton concentrations such that an increase in phytoplankton productivity results in an increase in turbidity and thus a decrease in photic zone depth

#### Life

- plankton
  - general term for group of organisms that drift in water, unable to propel themselves
  - individual is called plankter
  - divided into phytoplankton and zooplankton
    - phytoplankton
      - autotrophs (self-feeding), plant-like
    - zooplankton
      - predation, animal-like
- copepods
  - small crustaceans
  - found in nearly every freshwater and saltwater habitat
- nekton
  - animals that can propel themselves through water
  - includes fish, crabs, squids, etc.

### Mesopelagic

- depth: 656 - 3,280 feet
- AKA middle pelagic or twilight zone or dysphotic zone
- begins where 1% of incident light reaches, ends where there is no light
- includes regions with high gradients of differences in physical or chemical properties associated with depth
  - these are:
    - thermocline -> temperature
    - halocline -> salinity
    - pycnocline -> density
  - essentially, this is the transition zone between surface ocean water and deep ocean water
- SOFAR channel
  - located at the base of the mesopelagic
  - temperature determines the speed of sound in the ocean from the surface to the base of the mesopelagic (i.e., the base of the thermocline)
    - speed of sound is faster in warmer water (closer to the surface)
  - below the base of the mesopelagic, temperature stabilizes and pressure determines the speed of sound, increasing the speed as pressure increases
  - thus, the SOFAR channel the minimum point of sound speed and acts as a waveguide, restricting the direction of transmission of energy
  - low frequency sound waves within the channel might travel thousands of miles before dissipating
- important for water mass formation such as mode water (vertically homogeneous)
